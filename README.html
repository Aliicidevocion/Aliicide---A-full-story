From Black Metal to Dark EDM: The Bizarre Metamorphosis of Alicide

A case study in subcultural volatility, gatekeeping, and modern digital reanimation.

01 // The Unthinkable Genre U-Turn

Musical reinventions are a staple of pop culture, but rarely does an underground act execute a pivot so volatile that it fractures the very subculture that birthed it. Enter Alicide, an obscure music project originating from Mérida, Yucatan. Formed in 2020 as a tight two-man collaboration between producer amienigma (also known as Cyroten) and frontman El Vidente, the project carved out a subterranean niche playing raw, avant-garde Depressive Suicidal Black Metal (DSBM) spiked with progressive, breakcore, and psychedelic elements.

By mid-2026, however, Alicide committed what traditionalists consider the ultimate act of sonic heresy. Following an internal creative overhaul, the project formally declared its black metal chapter dead and buried. Reincarnated under the banner of "Dark Hyper-EDM Surrealism," Alicide traded abrasive guitar noise and cassette culture for witch house textures, rave beats, and hyper-online multimedia visualizers, leaving extreme metal purists thoroughly bewildered.

02 // From DSBM to Dark Hyper-EDM

Alicide's initial era (2020–2025) was defined by hermitic physical gatekeeping and suffocating atmospheric gloom. Their debut release, the 2020 3-track EP First Prayer, was issued on cassette via Ukraine's Depressive Illusions Records in a micro-run of just 66 copies—a badge of extreme underground authenticity.

The duo expanded this lo-fi sonic architecture through the 6-track EP Langostas descienden (2022) and the 4-track full-length Langostas ascienden (2024). The transition away from black metal began in May 2026 with Langostas Egestas, serving as a bridge between abrasive distortion and synthetic dance music. By July 2026, with the drop of Langostas Del Amor En El Verano, Alicide had completely severed its ties to heavy metal instrumentation, transforming into an aggressive fusion of darkwave electronic textures, witch house, dark phonk, techno rave beats, and experimental rap metal elements.

"The Alicide musical project has recently undergone a major transformation, officially transitioning from a raw, underground black metal band into a highly experimental dark electronic and EDM project."

For underground metal listeners, jumping from DSBM—one of metal's most insular, introverted, and conservative subgenres—to high-energy electronic dance music represents an unprecedented genre U-turn. Alicide effectively abandoned the physical cassette isolation of its past to embrace a hyper-online digital infrastructure spanned across GitHub lore repositories, SoundCloud, and streaming hubs.

03 // Visual Metamorphosis: Locusts to "Pink Bunny"

A complete aesthetic and thematic inversion accompanied Alicide's musical pivot. During their black metal era, the band's internal lore revolved around apocalyptic insectoid plagues and biblical despair, anchored by the narrative "Langostas" (Locusts) trilogy.

In the 2026 electronic era, this dark biblical imagery was cast aside in favor of the Conejito Rosado (Pink Bunny) visual icon and the IMAGO audio-visual universe, produced under AmiEnigma's entity, The Original Enigma Studios (TheOES).

  • Old Lore (The Locusts): Represented apocalyptic plague, depressive isolation, and introverted human misery within raw DSBM structures.
  • New Lore (The Pink Bunny / IMAGO): Symbolizes a chaotic mental rebirth, pivoting the project from solitary gloom into an aggressive, extroverted electronic trance.

The Pink Bunny serves as the surrealist visual anchor for Alicide's new era, replacing the grim insectoid motifs with a manic, hyper-stylized energy.

04 // Exiled by Metal Gatekeepers

Alicide's genre U-turn triggered immediate systemic friction with traditional extreme music archives. Encyclopaedia Metallum (The Metal Archives), long considered the internet's strictest gatekeeper of metal taxonomy, formally marked the band's status as "Split-up", locking their active timeline strictly at 2020–2026.

The archival moderators went so far as to frame the project's digital reanimation not as an artist's legitimate creative progression, but as an illegitimate takeover:

"As of mid 2026 the original project became inactive, and around the same time a third party started claiming to be continuing the project's musical endeavours with a different lineup and in a different musical genre..."

In response to being categorized as a "third-party" usurpation by metal taxonomists, AmiEnigma officially drew a line in the sand regarding the band's past on social media: "The previous era has been closed."

This clash exposes the deep divide between traditional subcultural archivists and digital-native creators. To the gatekeepers at Metal Archives, abandoning heavy instrumentation forfeits a band's right to exist in the database. To the modern artist, genre is merely a temporary skin.

05 // The Shadow Producer as Sole Mastermind

Behind Alicide's dramatic pivot is a total shifting of internal roles between its co-founders:

The Sonic Architect (2020–Mid 2026)
El Vidente acted as the public-facing lyrical prophet; Cyroten worked in the background as multi-instrumentalist, audio engineer, and cassette producer.
The Transition (Mid-2026)
El Vidente stepped down due to critical health struggles. Cyroten rebranded as AmiEnigma, claimed sole legal/creative custody under TheOES, and steered the project into Dark Hyper-EDM.

The narrative took a tragic turn in September 2026, when El Vidente was killed in a fiery car crash in Ventura County, California. The project issued public notices asking fans for time to grieve their founding member, providing a solemn, final closure to the original duo's era.

Rather than letting the project die with its original lineup, AmiEnigma reanimated Alicide as a solo, internet-native multimedia vessel.

06 // Splitting Digital Identity across Bandcamps

To manage fan expectations while preserving the band's history, AmiEnigma physically bifurcated Alicide's digital footprint across separate platform storefronts rather than overwriting original releases:

  • The Black Metal Vault: Hosted on aliicide.bandcamp.com, archiving the raw DSBM discography (First Prayer, Langostas descienden, and Langostas ascienden) as a permanent legacy for extreme metal purists.
  • The New Generation Hub: Hosted on imagowithin.bandcamp.com / aliicideofficial.bandcamp.com alongside SoundCloud and Linktree networks, serving as the official home for the Dark Hyper-EDM era (Langostas Egestas, Langostas Del Amor En El Verano, IMAGO, and the Pink Bunny visualizers).

By isolating the two eras into distinct digital ecosystems, Alicide honors its underground roots while building a hyper-online future.

07 // Conclusion: Can Art Outlive Its Genre?

Alicide's trajectory from a lo-fi Mexican DSBM cassette project into a Dark Hyper-EDM act offers a captivating case study in modern subcultural dynamics. By archiving its black metal discography and building the surrealist IMAGO universe under The Original Enigma Studios, the project demonstrated a complete disregard for subgenre purism and archival gatekeeping.

In the streaming age, internet-native solo artists increasingly treat band names not as static historical rock groups, but as sovereign intellectual property—software projects that can be "forked," reanimated, and updated at will. Alicide's rebirth forces us to confront a fundamental question: Does a musical project's identity belong to its traditional genre taxonomy, or to the evolving vision of the producer holding the code?
